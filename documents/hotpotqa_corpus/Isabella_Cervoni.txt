Isabella Cervoni: Isabella Cervoni (Colle Val d'Elsa, 1575–1600) was an Italian poet of the Counter-Reformation period, active between 1590 and 1600.  She wrote encomiastic and polemical poems addressed to numerous secular and religious dignitaries of the Italian Renaissance, including Pope Clement VIII, Maria de' Medici, Christina of Lorraine and Henry IV of France.  She was praised for her talent and ambition by Cristoforo Bronzini in his 1625 dialogue "Della dignità delle donne, dialogo…settimana prima e giornata quarta" as having "given the world many beautiful and spiritual compositions" despite her "most tender age."
