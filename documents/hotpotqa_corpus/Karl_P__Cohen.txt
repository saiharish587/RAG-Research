Karl P. Cohen: Karl Paley Cohen (February 5, 1913 – April 6, 2012) was a physical chemist who became a mathematical physicist and helped usher in the age of nuclear energy and reactor development.  He began his career in 1937 making scientific advances in uranium enrichment (isotope separation) as research assistant to Harold Urey, whose discovery of deuterium–the heavy isotope of hydrogen– earned him the 1934 Nobel Prize.  Cohen worked within the Columbia group of physicists that included Harold Urey, Enrico Fermi, Leo Szilard, Isidor Isaac Rabi, John R. Dunning, Eugene T. Booth, A. Von Gross and others)–all pioneers of nuclear energy.
