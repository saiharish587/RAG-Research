Rose engine lathe: A rose engine lathe is a specialized kind of geometric lathe.  The headstock rocks back and forth with a rocking motion or along the spindle axis in a pumping motion, controlled by a rubber moving against a rosette or cam-like pattern mounted on the spindle, while the lathe spindle rotates.  Rose engine work can make flower patterns, as well as convoluted, symmetrical, multi-lobed geometric patterns.  The patterns it produces are similar to that of a Spirograph, in metal.  No other ornamental lathe can produce these "rose" patterns.  The decoration produced by a rose engine lathe is called guilloche.  It sometimes confused with "jewel finishes" or engine turning, a much cheaper process of making swirly marks in metal by a rotating abrasive peg or pad, which is repeatedly applied to the surface to make a pattern of overlapping circles.  Jewel finishes used to be common on stereo faceplates and automobile interiors.
