Izhitsa: Izhitsa (Ѵ, ѵ; OCS Ѵжица, Russian: И́жица ) is a letter of the early Cyrillic alphabet and several later alphabets, usually the last in the row.  It originates from the Greek letter upsilon (Y, υ) and was used in words and names derived from or via the Greek language, such as кѵрилъ ("kürilǔ", "Cyril") or флаѵии ("flavii", "Flavius").  It represented the sounds /<a href="">i/ or /<a href="">v/ as normal letters и and в, respectively.  The Glagolitic alphabet has a corresponding letter with the name "izhitsa" as well (Ⱛ, ⱛ).  Also, izhitsa in its standard form or, most often, in a tailed variant (similar to Latin "y") was a part of a digraph оѵ/оу representing sound /<a href="">u/ .  The digraph is known as Cyrillic "uk", and today's Cyrillic letter u originates from its simplified form.
