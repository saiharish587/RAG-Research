Gender essentialism: In feminist theory and gender studies, gender essentialism is the attribution of a fixed essence to women.  Women's essence is assumed to be universal and is generally identified with those characteristics viewed as being specifically feminine.  These ideas of femininity are usually biologized are often preoccupied with psychological characteristics, such as nurturance, empathy, support, non-competitiveness, etc.  Feminist theorist Elizabeth Grosz states in her 1995 publication, "Space, time and perversion: essays on the politics of bodies", that essentialism "entails the belief that those characteristics defined as women's essence are shared in common by all women at all times.  It implies a limit of the variations and possibilities of change—it is not possible for a subject to act in a manner contrary to her essence.  Her essence underlies all the apparent variations differentiating women from each other.  Essentialism thus refers to the existence of fixed characteristic, given attributes, and ahistorical functions that limit the possibilities of change and thus of social reorganization."
