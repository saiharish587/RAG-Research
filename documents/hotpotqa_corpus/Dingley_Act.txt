Dingley Act: The Dingley Act of 1897 (ch.  11, 30 Stat.  151 , July 24, 1897), introduced by U.S. Representative Nelson Dingley, Jr., of Maine, raised tariffs in United States to counteract the Wilson–Gorman Tariff Act of 1894, which had lowered rates.  Came into effect under William McKinley the first year that he was in office.  The McKinley administration wanted to slowly bring back the protectionism that was proposed by the Tariff of 1890.
