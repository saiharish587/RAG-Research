1966 Atlantic hurricane season: The 1966 Atlantic hurricane season featured the tropical cyclone with the longest track in the Atlantic basin – Hurricane Faith.  Also during the year, the Miami, Florida Weather Office was re-designated the National Hurricane Center.  The season officially began on June 1, and lasted until November 30.  These dates conventionally delimit the period of each year when most tropical cyclones form in the Atlantic basin.  It was a near average season in terms of tropical storms, with a total of 11 named storms.  The first system, Hurricane Alma, developed over eastern Nicaragua on June 4.  Alma brought severe flooding to Honduras and later to Cuba, after crossing the western Caribbean Sea.  The storm also brought relatively minor impact to the Southeastern United States.  Alma caused 91 deaths and about $210.1 million (1966 USD) in damage.
