Aceria clianthi: Aceria clianthi is a species of mite belonging to the family Eriophyidae.  It is found only in New Zealand.  It is notable for being host specific to threatened plants of the genus "Clianthus".  It is classified by Buckley "et al." as "nationally critical" under the New Zealand Threat Classification System.  They stated ""Aceria clianthi" (Eriophyidae), has been recorded only from kakabeak ("Clianthus" spp.)  in cultivation and once on "Lotus cornalatus" ["Lotus corniculatus"] (Fabaceae), an introduced plant growing near kakabeak (Martin 2009).  It is given the same threat classification as kakabeak (de Lange et al. 2009)."  Heenan had earlier stated that "the two species [of "Clianthus"] are considered to be threatened, with "C. maximus" having a rank of vulnerable, whereas "C. puniceus" is critically endangered", but the conservation status of "C. maximus" was subsequently found to be more serious.  These threat classifications for "Clianthus" apply to plants in the wild, but the species are widely cultivated.  "Aceria clianthi" occurs on both plants in the wild and in cultivation.
