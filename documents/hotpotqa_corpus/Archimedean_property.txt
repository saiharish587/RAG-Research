Archimedean property: In abstract algebra and analysis, the Archimedean property, named after the ancient Greek mathematician Archimedes of Syracuse, is a property held by some algebraic structures, such as ordered or normed groups, and fields.  Roughly speaking, it is the property of having no "infinitely large" or "infinitely small" elements.  It was Otto Stolz who gave the axiom of Archimedes its name because it appears as Axiom V of Archimedes’ "On the Sphere and Cylinder".
