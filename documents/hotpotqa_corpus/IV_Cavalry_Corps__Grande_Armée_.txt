IV Cavalry Corps (Grande Armée): The IV Cavalry Corps (Grande Armée) was a French military formation that existed during the Napoleonic Wars.  The corps was created in 1812 and rebuilt in 1813 and 1815.  Emperor Napoleon first organized the corps for the French invasion of Russia.  Under General of Division Victor de Fay de La Tour-Maubourg, the corps fought at Borodino.  During the War of the Sixth Coalition in 1813, General of Division François Étienne de Kellermann commanded the all-Polish corps at Leipzig.  During the Hundred Days in 1815, Napoleon reconstituted the corps and nominated General of Division Édouard Jean Baptiste Milhaud to direct it.  Composed entirely of cuirassier regiments, the two divisions fought at Ligny and Waterloo.
