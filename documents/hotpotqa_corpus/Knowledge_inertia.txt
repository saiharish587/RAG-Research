Knowledge inertia: Knowledge inertia (KI) is a concept in knowledge management.  The term initially proposed by Liao (2002) constitutes a two dimensional model of knowledge inertia which incorporates experience inertia and learning inertia.  Later, another dimension—the dimension of thinking inertia has been added based on the theoretical exploration of the existing concepts of experience inertia and learning inertia.  One of the central problems in knowledge management related to organizational learning is to deal with “inertia”.  Besides, individuals may also exhibit a natural tendency of inertia when facing problems during utilization of knowledge.  Inertia in technical jargon means inactivity or torpor.  Inertia in organizational learning context may be referred to as a slowdown in organizational learning-related activities.  In fact, there are many other kinds of organizational inertia; e.g., innovation inertia, workforce inertia, productivity inertia, decision inertia, emotional inertia besides others that have different meanings in their own individual contexts.  Some organization theorists have adopted the definition proposed by Liao (2002) to extend its further use in organizational learning studies.
