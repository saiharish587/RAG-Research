Near polygon: In mathematics, a near polygon is an incidence geometry introduced by Ernest E. Shult and Arthur Yanushka in 1980.  Shult and Yanushka showed the connection between the so-called tetrahedrally closed line-systems in Euclidean spaces and a class of point-line geometries which they called near polygons.  These structures generalise the notion of generalized polygon as every generalized 2"n"-gon is a near 2"n"-gon of a particular kind.  Near polygons were extensively studied and connection between them and dual polar spaces was shown in 1980s and early 1990s.  Some sporadic simple groups, for example the Hall-Janko group and the Mathieu groups, act as automorphism groups of near polygons.
