Federal Act on Banks and Savings Banks: The Federal Act on Banks and Savings Banks (colloquially known as the Banking Law of 1934) is the law that governs the regulation of banks in Switzerland.  It created the current regime of bank secrecy that Swiss banks are famous for.  Under the Law, it is a criminal act for a bank to reveal the name of an account holder.  The law helped establish Switzerland as the premier tax haven for foreigners, with Swiss banks accounting for approximately US$2.1 trillion in offshore assets, 27% of the world's total.  Since then, Swiss banks have acquired a reputation because of their numbered bank accounts, which critics such as ATTAC NGO alleged facilitate tax evasion, money laundering and more generally the underground economy.
