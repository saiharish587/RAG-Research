Grafton Elliot Smith: Smith was born in Grafton, New South Wales to Stephen Sheldrick Smith who had moved to Australia from London in 1860 and Mary Jane, née Evans.  He received his early education from Grafton Public School where his father was headmaster.  When the family moved to Sydney in 1883 he went to Darlington Public School before joining Sydney Boys High School.  He attended evening classes on physiology by Thomas Anderson Stuart and took an early interest in biology.  In an autobiographical note Smith noted that Stuart had shown them the convolutions of the human brain and declared that nobody understood them fully.  Smith decided at that point that he would work towards understanding them.  Accordingly, he went to study medicine at the University of Sydney in 1888 and received a Doctor of Medicine in 1895, with a dissertation on the fore-brain of the monotremes) and developed an interest in the anatomy of the human brain.  He received a James King travelling scholarship and went to St John's College, Cambridge, graduating BA in 1896.  Afterwards he catalogued the human brain-collection of the British Museum.
