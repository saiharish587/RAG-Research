Transstadial transmission: Transstadial transmission occurs when a pathogen remains with the vector from one life stage ("stadium") to the next.  For example, the bacteria "Borrelia burgdorferi", the causative agent for Lyme disease, infects the tick vector as a larva, and the infection is maintained when it molts to a nymph and later develops as an adult.  This type of transmission is seen in other parasites like viruses or "Rickettsia".  In addition to ticks, mites are another common vector.  Some sources consider transstadial transmission a type of horizontal transmission, whereas other sources consider it vertical or partial vertical transmission.
