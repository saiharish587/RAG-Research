Kempf vanishing theorem: In algebraic geometry, the Kempf vanishing theorem, introduced by , states that the higher cohomology group "H"("G"/"B","L"(λ)) ("i" > 0) vanishes whenever λ is a dominant weight of "B".  Here "G" is a reductive algebraic group over an algebraically closed field, "B" a Borel subgroup, and "L"(λ) a line bundle associated to λ.  In characteristic 0 this is a special case of the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem, but unlike the Borel–Weil–Bott theorem, the Kempf vanishing theorem still holds in positive characteristic.
