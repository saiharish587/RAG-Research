Tonality: Tonality is the arrangement of pitches and/or chords of a musical work in a hierarchy of perceived relations, stabilities, attractions and directionality.  In this hierarchy, the individual pitch or triadic chord with the greatest stability is called the tonic.  The root of the tonic chord forms the name given to the key; so in the key of C major, the note C is both the tonic of the scale and the root of the tonic chord (C–E–G).  Simple folk music songs often start and end with the tonic note.  The most common use of the term "is to designate the arrangement of musical phenomena around a referential tonic in European music from about 1600 to about 1910" .  Contemporary classical music from 1910 to the 2000s may practice or avoid any sort of tonality—but harmony in almost all Western popular music remains tonal.  Harmony in jazz includes many but not all tonal characteristics of the European common practice period, sometimes known as "classical music".
