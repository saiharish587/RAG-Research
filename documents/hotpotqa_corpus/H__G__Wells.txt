H. G. Wells: Herbert George Wells (21 September 1866 – 13 August 1946), usually referred to as H. G. Wells, was an English writer.  He was prolific in many genres, including the novel, history, politics, social commentary, and textbooks and rules for war games.  Wells is now best remembered for his science fiction novels and is called a "father of science fiction", along with Jules Verne and Hugo Gernsback.  His most notable science fiction works include "The Time Machine" (1895), "The Island of Doctor Moreau" (1896), "The Invisible Man" (1897), and "The War of the Worlds" (1898).  He was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature four times.
