Saint John's Cathedral Boys' School: Saint John's Cathedral Boys' School (SJCBS) was a school that was located in Selkirk, Manitoba.  It was founded in the early 1960s by Ted Byfield and Frank Wiens.  The two started an Anglican lay order called the Company of the Cross, claimed to be based on a reading of some of C.S. Lewis's writings.  The Company of the Cross was under the authority of the resident bishop in Winnipeg, officially called the Diocese of Rupert's Land.  The teachers were paid $1.00 per day and provided room and board.  Two other schools, Saint John's School of Alberta and Saint John's School of Ontario were founded on the same ideas in later years.  Arduous row-boat trips (called "cutters"), later replaced by canoes, and snowshoeing and dog-sledding were part of the outdoor education program.  The school's founders believed that boys should be pushed to what they might believe is their breaking points, and this would "build character".  The school was seen by many as a way to help troubled boys, usually from 11 to 14 years of age.  Its primary focus was challenging boys from every social stratum to work together in order to grow morally, physically, intellectually and spiritually in the tradition of Victorian "muscular Christianity".
